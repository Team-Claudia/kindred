### I1 — Priya, 44 (synthetic)

**Moderator:** Are you a caregiver, and do you share responsibilities with someone else?

**Priya:** Yes. Well, I'm *the* caregiver, if you want the honest version. I look after my mom, and on paper I share it with my two brothers. In practice... we'll get to that, I'm sure.

**Moderator:** Tell us about the person you care for.

**Priya:** My mom, Kamala. She's 76. She was diagnosed with Parkinson's about six years ago, and the last two years it's really picked up. Her walking is shuffly, she freezes in doorways, and her voice has gone quiet, so phone calls are hard because you can't always hear her. She lives alone in a townhouse in Newton, about fifteen minutes from me. She won't move. We've had that fight more than once. She says, "Your father and I bought this place," and that's the end of it.

She's still sharp, mostly. Some days she's foggy, usually when her meds are off. She's proud. She doesn't like being "managed", which is a bit of a problem when your daughter manages people for a living.

**Moderator:** What's your current status? Are you working, retired or studying?

**Priya:** Working full-time. I'm a nurse manager at a hospital, rotating shifts, so some weeks I'm on days and some weeks I'm covering evenings. That's part of why it's hard. My schedule changes every two weeks and nobody in my family can keep track of it. Honestly, I can barely keep track of it.

**Moderator:** How many people share this care with you? How do you split responsibility between you?

**Priya:** So, three of us. Me, my older brother Arjun, who's in Calgary, and my younger brother Vikram, who's here, in Surrey, actually closer to Mom than I am.

Vikram "helps when asked". That's literally how he'd describe it. If I text him, "Can you take Mom to the pharmacy Thursday," he'll usually do it. But he doesn't *see* anything. He's never once called me and said, "Hey, I noticed Mom's running low on this." I'm the one who notices.

And then there's a neighbour, Doreen, who checks on her. Not family, but honestly more reliable than some family.

**Moderator:** Is there a main caregiver, or does everyone split the tasks fairly evenly?

**Priya:** [laughs] No. No, it's not even. I'm the main one. I do the medications, I book the appointments, I talk to the neurologist's office, I deal with the home care assessment people, I do the forms. Arjun does money, Vikram does rides when I ask him. If I disappeared tomorrow I genuinely don't know who would know her doctor's name.

And I'll be honest, I resent it a bit. More than a bit some weeks. I manage a unit all day and then I come home and manage my family. Partly it's because I'm a nurse, so everyone assumes I'm the obvious one. And partly it's because I'm the daughter. Nobody says that out loud, but it's there.

**Moderator:** Tell me about a time something slipped through the cracks.

**Priya:** Oh, I have a good one. Well, a bad one.

Vikram didn't tell anyone. He says the doctor "said something about the pills" and he figured it'd come through the pharmacy. Mom thought Vikram was going to tell me. **Moderator:** How did that make you feel, in the moment?

**Priya:** Furious. Then guilty, because I wasn't there. Then furious again. I'm a nurse. This is literally the kind of thing I catch at work. And I missed it with my own mother because I wasn't in the room and nobody thought it was their job to tell me.

**Moderator:** How did it get resolved? What would help you avoid something like that happening again?

**Priya:** I called the pharmacy that afternoon and sorted the packs. I called the clinic Monday to confirm. She was fine, in the end. No fall, thank God. But it could've been a fall.

What would help? Honestly, I don't know. Part of me thinks the only fix is that I go to every appointment, which isn't possible. Maybe if there was a set list of questions whoever goes has to answer. **Moderator:** How do you assign tasks to different caregivers?

**Priya:** I text. Mostly I text Vikram directly, not in the group, because if I put it in the group it's like shouting into a void. Everyone reacts with a thumbs-up and nobody actually commits. If I text him directly, "Can you do Thursday at two," he has to answer.

**Moderator:** What happens when someone can't make it to their caregiving duty? What do you do now?

**Priya:** It comes back to me. Every time. Vikram cancels, usually the night before, sometimes the morning of. Work thing, kid thing, whatever. And then I'm either swapping a shift, which as a manager I can sometimes do but it costs me favours, or I call Doreen next door, or I book a cab and phone Mom to make sure she's ready.

Once I just had to cancel a physio appointment because nobody could go. It took five weeks to get another one.

To be fair to Vikram, it's not constant. Maybe one in four times? I don't know. It feels like more because I remember the bad ones.

**Moderator:** How do you share a new update with the others in your care circle?

**Priya:** WhatsApp group. It's called "Mom" with a little flower emoji. Arjun's wife is in it too, and my husband. Mom isn't in it. We tried, she found it overwhelming.

So I post updates there. But the problem is it's a mix of everything. A photo of Mom's tomatoes, a forwarded video from Arjun's wife, then me saying "Neuro appointment moved to the 14th," and then forty messages later nobody can find the appointment date. I've pinned messages. It doesn't help much.

For anything serious I phone. Like when she had a fall in January, I phoned both brothers. I don't put that kind of thing in a group chat first.

**Moderator:** After an appointment, how does everyone find out what happened?

**Priya:** If I went, I write a summary in the group. A proper one, because I'm a nurse, I can't help it. Meds, any changes, next steps, next appointment.

If someone else went, it's what I said before: "Went fine." And then I phone Mom to get the real story, and Mom tells me a slightly different version, and sometimes I end up calling the clinic myself. The clinic staff know my voice now.

So, basically, everyone finds out what happened through me. Even when I wasn't there.

**Moderator:** Is it easy or difficult to share a medical update in the family? Why?

**Priya:** Difficult. Partly the jargon. If I say "they're worried about orthostatic hypotension," my brothers glaze over. So I translate: her blood pressure drops when she stands, she's more likely to fall. And then that takes three paragraphs.

And partly emotional. Arjun doesn't want bad news. When I tell him something's getting worse, he gets defensive, like I'm exaggerating, or he wants to jump in with solutions, "Should we get a specialist in Vancouver?" From Calgary. And Vikram just goes quiet.

And there's Mom's privacy. She doesn't always want the boys to know things. Bladder stuff, for example. So I'm filtering. I'm deciding what each person gets told. That's exhausting, actually, now that I say it out loud.

**Moderator:** If you could fix one thing about how your family coordinates, what would it be?

**Priya:** That I'm not the single point of failure. That's the nurse manager in me talking. If I'm on nights, or sick, or, God forbid, I want to go on holiday, the whole thing should keep working. Right now it doesn't. All the information lives in my head and my phone.

If I'm being really honest, maybe the fix is my brothers stepping up, and no amount of organising fixes that. But I'd settle for them at least being able to see what's going on without asking me.

**Moderator:** Imagine a magic phone app that solves all your caregiving problems. How would it help you, and why?

**Priya:** Magic? Okay. One place where Mom's current meds and schedule live, and it's the real current one, not the one from three months ago. Upcoming appointments with who's taking her, and if that person cancels, it nags *them* to find a replacement, not me.

After an appointment, whoever went has to fill in what changed. Simple questions, not a free-text box, because Vikram will write "fine."

And I'd want to be able to see my shift schedule against hers. Like, it knows I'm on evenings next week, so it doesn't assume I can do the Tuesday.

Oh, and the brothers could look things up themselves. "When's Mom's next neuro?" Look it up. Don't text me.

But I'll say this. I've seen apps for this kind of thing come up in nursing newsletters and I've never downloaded one, because I know my brothers won't use it. Getting Vikram to install something new is harder than getting Mom to move. So the magic part would really be that they actually use it.

**Moderator:** How much would you pay for an app that helps with task coordination and keeping everyone aligned, with AI summaries that keep you up to date? $1 to $10, $10 to $20, $20 to $30, or $30 or more per month?

**Priya:** Hmm. Money isn't really the issue for us. If it genuinely worked, if it took even a quarter of this off my plate, I'd pay ten to twenty a month without blinking. Honestly I'd make Arjun pay for it, that's his department. [laughs]

But I'd want to try it free first, with the whole family, for a month or so. Because if the boys don't use it, it's worth zero to me. And the AI summaries, I'd be a bit careful. If it summarises a neurologist's note wrong, that's worse than no summary. I'd want to see what it's summarising from.

So ten to twenty. Only if the rest of the family's actually in it.
