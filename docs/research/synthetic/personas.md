# Synthetic personas

> **Synthetic data.** These people are fictional, written to drive AI-generated responses. They are not research participants. See [README](README.md).

The mix was set before any answers were made, so the answers don't just echo our assumptions. About a third report little or no coordination pain, some care alone or with no main coordinator, and several would not pay.

## Interview personas

All six pass the interview screener: they care (now or before) for a family member and share it with at least one sibling or relative.

| ID | Persona |
|---|---|
| I1 | **Priya, 44, Surrey BC.** Nurse manager, full-time, rotating shifts. Cares for her mother (76, Parkinson's, lives alone in a townhouse). Two brothers: one in Calgary who sends money, one local who "helps when asked". Priya is the default coordinator and resents it. WhatsApp group plus phone calls. Comfortable financially. Talkative, frank, a little tired. |
| I2 | **Marcus, 52, Victoria BC.** Self-employed renovation contractor. Father (81) has early dementia and still lives in the family home. His younger sister lives five minutes away and does most of the work; Marcus does repairs, driving and weekends. Texts and calls only; sceptical of apps ("one more thing to check"). Brief answers, needs prompting. |
| I3 | **Linh, 38, Vancouver BC.** Software product manager, full-time, hybrid. Both parents live in Richmond; her father had a stroke last year. One sister in Toronto. They already use a shared Google Calendar and a Notion page. Scheduling mostly works; the hard part is information, because her parents talk to doctors in Vietnamese and details get lost. Analytical, quick to critique tools. |
| I4 | **Denise, 63, Kelowna BC.** Retired teacher. Past caregiver: cared for her mother through cancer until she died in 2024. Four siblings, scattered across BC and Alberta; one brother rarely helped and they still don't speak much. Used phone trees and a paper binder by the phone. Reflective, emotional at times, long stories. |
| I5 | **Raj, 47, Burnaby BC.** Part-time accountant (cut his hours to help). Father-in-law (79) has diabetes and heart failure and lives with Raj's family. Raj's wife and her two siblings share care on a fixed weekly rota posted on the fridge and in a WhatsApp group. It works well; they rarely argue. Practical, mildly amused by the question of paying for an app. |
| I6 | **Hannah, 29, Nanaimo BC.** Retail assistant manager, full-time. Helps care for her grandmother (88, frailty and hearing loss) with her mother and her aunt. Hannah is the youngest and handles anything digital: booking appointments online, the pharmacy app. Money is tight. Friendly, candid, a bit scattered. |
