# Secondary research: Caregiver Tech Insights Survey 2026

> **Published research by another organization, not our own data.** Cite it as "a 2026 survey of 272 US family caregivers found…", never as "we found…".

## Source

Caregiver Action Network (CAN), *Shaping the Future of Caregiving Technology: Caregiver Tech Insights Survey, Findings Report 2026*. [PDF](https://www.caregiveraction.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/CIC-Survey-2026.pdf), published May 2026.

## Method and limits

- **Who:** 272 family caregivers from CAN's email list, a US caregiver nonprofit. 12 respondents were drawn at random for $50 gift cards.
- **Margin of error:** ±5.94% at 95% confidence.
- **Respondents:** the largest age group was 35–49 (38%), and most had been caring for 5+ years. Parents and in-laws were the most common care recipients (40%), then spouses and partners (25%). Top conditions: Alzheimer's or cognitive impairment, general aging, diabetes.

What this means for us:

- **US, not Canada.** Costs and coverage differ, especially anything about Medicare or insurance. For the Canadian picture we still use Statistics Canada and *Caring in Canada*.
- **Not sibling-specific.** It doesn't ask about sharing care with siblings, who coordinates, or family conflict. It backs up the coordination burden, not our sibling angle.
- **Self-selected and experienced.** People on a caregiver nonprofit's email list are likely more engaged and more tech-ready than the average caregiver.

### Caregivers in their own words

Anonymised quotes from the report:

- "The most stressful part of caregiving is trying to coordinate everything at once, appointments, medications, and daily care, while still managing my own responsibilities." (p. 12)
- "Handling it all alone, not having a roadmap, or needing to search for resources, not knowing what is next or what my next steps are." (p. 12)
- "Shared calendars, like Google Calendar, help track appointments between multiple family members." (p. 11)
- "One simple app that automatically organizes medical records, appointments, medications, and doctor instructions in one place for caregivers and family." (p. 13)
- "Just a phone to call people. Wish automated crap would go away." (p. 11)

The report's own conclusion: *"Caregivers are stitching together partial tools while still carrying the full coordination burden."* (p. 14)

## What it means for Kindred

**Confirms**

- Coordination is the heavy part of caregiving, and it's measurable: 37% spend 11+ hours a week on it.
- Information is scattered across tools and people. Caregivers want one place that the family shares.
- Ease of setup decides adoption. Almost a quarter would rather use text or email than an app, which supports helpers joining free with one tap and requests shared to the family chat.
- Caregivers trust organizations. Introductions to Family Caregivers of BC and Alzheimer Society chapters (our ask) fit the report's #1 adoption factor.

**Challenges**

- **Willingness to pay.** Only 12% would pay out of pocket, and 32% expect it free. Our $5 founding price is below the $11–25 band that 28% would pay, and helpers never pay, but we should expect resistance. The pilot has to prove value before the founding price ends. Over time, employer caregiver benefits or partner-funded access are worth looking into.
- **Trust and privacy** are almost as big a barrier as cost, so our privacy message needs to be plain and early.
- **Safety monitoring** was the single top priority (28%). Kindred doesn't do this, and won't.

**Compared with our synthetic pilot** ([docs/research/synthetic](../synthetic/summary.md))

- **Both point the same way on:** coordination pain, getting information to the right people after appointments (the report's 72% for visit summaries matches the synthetic personas' top theme), ease of setup, and caution about trust.
- **They differ on price.** 20 of 30 synthetic respondents would pay something, but only 12% of real respondents would pay out of pocket. The synthetic answers were probably too optimistic, so trust the report here.

## Roadmap idea (not built for Demo Day)

**A "What changed?" step when posting an appointment update.** After an appointment, Kindred asks whoever went a few yes/no questions: any medication changes, a new appointment, anything the family should know. The answers go to everyone. The family writes them, not AI, and nothing in it is medical advice, so it stays within "Never clinical". Evidence: 72% value visit summaries (p. 6), plus the medication-handoff stories in the synthetic interviews.
