# Kindred — Go-to-Market Plan

Version 3, October 2026. productBC buildathon. Launch market: Canada.

## 1. Summary

**What this plan is for:** it sets out how we'll take Kindred from buildathon prototype to its first 100 paying families in Canada: who we target, what we say, how we reach them, what we charge, and how we'll know it's working. It guides our Demo Day pitch, our first channel test, and the decisions after it.

**Kindred in one line:** a task-coordination app that gives siblings one shared plan for caring for a parent, so every task has an owner, care doesn't slip, and no one carries it alone.

## 2. Product–market fit

### Product: the problem we solve

When several family members share a parent's care, nobody has one clear view of who is doing what. Appointments and medications get missed or doubled up, one person quietly absorbs most of the work, and the gaps turn into resentment, blame and arguments. Care becomes reactive, moving from crisis to crisis, and its quality drops over time. Kindred gives the family one shared plan where every task has an owner who has claimed it.

### Market: who our customers are

Siblings and relatives in Canada, typically working adults aged 28–55, who share the care of a parent who lives alone and now depends on them.

### Fit: why they buy

- **Ease of use:** simpler than a spreadsheet; a non-techy relative joins in minutes.
- **Routine out of chaos:** a shared plan replaces crisis-by-crisis texting.
- **Accountability:** everyone sees who owns what, and when.
- **Conflict resolution:** a neutral record replaces "I do everything" arguments.
- **Works with existing apps:** connects to the calendars and messaging the family already uses.

## 3. Positioning

### 3.1 Ideal customer profile

| Role | Who |
|---|---|
| Buyer | The primary care coordinator: usually an adult child or sibling balancing work, family and care |
| Users | The wider Care Circle, typically 3–6 people: siblings, relatives, friends, neighbours and paid support workers |
| Care recipient | A single older adult who lives alone and now depends on others, after a fall, stroke, dementia diagnosis or hospital discharge |
| Geography | Canada |

### 3.2 Problem and triggers

What makes them look for a solution:

- A health event or hospital discharge.
- A missed appointment or medication.
- A family argument over who does what.

The pain underneath:

- Conflict between caregivers: unequal load, resentment and blame.
- Disagreement over how others handle care.
- Care quality drops over time: missed or duplicated care and coordinator burnout.

### 3.3 How they solve it today

- Short-term, temporary fixes, reacting to the latest crisis rather than planning ahead.
- Or doing nothing: one person quietly absorbs it all until they burn out.

Tools they lean on along the way:

| Tool | Where it breaks |
|---|---|
| WhatsApp or group text | Tasks get buried; no clear owner or status |
| Personal calendars | Schedules are separate; finding a free person means asking around |
| To-do apps, notes, spreadsheets | Not built for care; hard to share and hand off |
| Fridge calendar or memory | Only works for whoever is standing in the kitchen |
| Phone calls after appointments | Information stays with the person who went |

### 3.4 Who won't buy

- Single carers with no Care Circle to coordinate with.
- Independent older adults who don't yet need coordinated care.
- Low-conflict families happy with one group chat.
- Families fully outsourcing care to a paid agency or professional care manager.
- Families in end-of-life care who need clinical support.
- Families whose key relatives won't use a smartphone app.
- People outside Canada, for now.

### 3.5 Why buy from us, not something else

| Instead of… | Kindred gives them |
|---|---|
| A group chat | Every task has a named owner who claimed it, not a thumbs-up |
| A shared calendar | Not just when things happen, but who agreed and who can cover |
| A spreadsheet or to-do app | Built for care, and simple enough for every relative |
| Doing it all yourself | A shared plan, so the load is visible and shared |

### Positioning statement

For siblings sharing the care of a parent who depends on them, Kindred is the task-coordination app that gives the whole family one shared plan. Unlike group chats and calendars, every task has a named owner who claimed it, so care stops slipping and no one carries it alone.

## 6. Messaging

### 6.1 Why: why the customer should care

- Missed care has serious consequences: missed medications, appointments and visits.
- Accountability reduces conflict between caregivers.
- Transparency lifts the mental burden on the coordinator: no more chasing people.

### 6.2 How: how we deliver it

The flow: invite the Care Circle, set up recurring care needs, members claim tasks, and reminders keep it on track.

- **Clear responsibilities:** every task is documented and visible to all.
- **Shared accountability:** every task has an owner who has claimed it.
- **Visible availability:** see who can cover what, and spot gaps early.

### 6.3 What and proof

Features that make it work:

- **Shared care calendar:** every visit, appointment and task with a named owner.
- **Claim, accept and ask for cover:** tasks are claimed in one tap and handed off when plans change.
- **Push notifications and scheduling prompts:** open slots get claimed and nothing goes overdue unnoticed.
- **Works with existing apps:** Google Calendar sync, WhatsApp sharing, and AI summaries of updates.

Proof we'll collect from pilot families:

| Proof point | How we measure it |
|---|---|
| % of care slots covered | Tasks with a confirmed owner before their due date |
| Fewer missed tasks | Overdue tasks per week, against the family's own estimate at onboarding |
| Fewer coordinator check-ins | "How many times did you chase someone this week?" asked at onboarding and week 4 |
| Shared load | % of tasks completed by someone other than the coordinator |

### 6.4 Messages by audience

| Audience | Message |
|---|---|
| Coordinator | Stop being the only one who remembers. |
| Sibling or helper | Know exactly when it's your turn. |
| Less tech-comfortable relative | One tap: yes or no. |

### Objection handling

| They say | We say |
|---|---|
| "We have WhatsApp." | Keep it. Kindred tracks who's doing what and shares back into the chat. |
| "My brother won't use another app." | He just taps Accept. |
| "Why pay when the group chat is free?" | Founding families get their money back if Kindred doesn't help in 30 days. |
| "Is our information safe?" | Only people you invite can see your Care Circle. |

## 7. Pricing

Paid from day one. The coordinator pays per Care Circle; helpers always join free.

| Offer | Price | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Founding family | $5/month, locked in | First 100 families; 30-day money-back guarantee; personal onboarding |
| Kindred | $7/month or $70/year per circle | Standard price after the founding offer closes |
| Helpers | Free | Anyone the coordinator invites |

## 8. Growth: one-channel bet

### 8.1 First 100 customers

- Start with 10–20 families, onboarded by hand and paying from day one.
- Find them in caregiver communities (Facebook, Reddit), hospital caregiver and help groups, and elder-care organizations such as Family Caregivers of BC, Alzheimer Society chapters and seniors' centres.
- Each coordinator invites 3–5 relatives, who see Kindred in action and may start circles of their own.

### 8.2 Primary channel

- **Primary:** online caregiver communities in Canada: Facebook groups, plus Reddit (r/AgingParents, r/caregivers). Coordinators ask for help there at the moment of crisis.
- **Secondary:** hospital help groups and elder-care organizations. Slower, as they need partnerships.

### 8.3 How we win there

- Founder-led, helper not advertiser: join 10 groups, follow the rules, answer real coordination questions.
- Offer a free "who does what" care roster template that leads into Kindred.
- Work with group admins to share the template officially.
- Offer a free 1:1 setup to the first 20 circles who reach out, and collect weekly feedback.
- Share real stories: sibling conflict before, a shared plan after (with permission).

### 8.4 Success criteria

| | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **2-week test** | 10 groups joined, 20+ helpful posts, 30 conversations, 15 coordinators reached |
| **Continue if** | 5+ Care Circles set up, each with 3+ members; 1+ task completed by a non-coordinator in week one; 2+ families have paid |
| **Pivot if** | Fewer than 3 of 30 conversations lead to a trial |

### 8.5 Budget

| Period | Budget | Spent on |
|---|---|---|
| 2-week test | $0 | Founder time only (~5 hours a week) |
| Next 4–6 weeks | ~$500–1,000 CAD | Template design, interview thank-you gift cards, small boosted posts |
| After that | To be set | Paid ads only once messaging converts |

## 11. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Helpers don't join | One-tap Accept from a WhatsApp link; invite message written for siblings |
| A group chat feels good enough | Reach families right after a trigger; work with the chat, not against it |
| Paying for an early product leads to refunds | Founding-family price, money-back guarantee, hands-on onboarding |
| Promotion gets us removed from groups | Follow each group's rules; help first; partner with admins |
| People hesitate to share their calendar | Clear consent when connecting; disconnect any time |
| Concerns about care information and AI | Plain-language privacy policy, consent for AI features; PIPEDA review |
| iPhone notifications need Home Screen install | Guided install step during onboarding |
| Liability if a task is missed | Clear terms: Kindred coordinates tasks; it isn't medical advice |
